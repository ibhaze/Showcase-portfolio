ABOUT PROJECT

This project explored the theme of polarisation, focusing on how social media platforms influence emotional wellbeing and division among young people. The goal was to define a clear problem and propose a potential solution that promotes awareness and healthier online interactions.

What began as an abstract challenge quickly evolved into a deep reflection on how social media algorithms, engagement-driven design, and instant gratification contribute to polarisation. The project led to the creation of PARITY, a concept app aimed at fostering open, moderated conversations while making users conscious of how digital content affects their emotions.

MAIN PROBLEM

Social media originally aimed to connect people, but the introduction of features like likes, retweets, and algorithmic newsfeeds transformed it into a system that thrives on engagement often rewarding outrage and divisiveness. This business model doesn’t necessarily make social media evil, but it incentivizes content that triggers strong emotions, further deepening social and political divides.

Through research inspired by Tristan Harris and studies on psychological triggers and instant gratification, the project identified that this engagement-driven model negatively impacts users’ emotional wellbeing, particularly among young adults (18–35) who are highly active online and increasingly exposed to polarising content.

END RESULTS

The final concept, PARITY, is a digital platform designed to reduce polarisation and encourage balanced dialogue. The app creates a controlled space where people can share opinions, see multiple perspectives, and engage in moderated discussions that avoid hate speech or extreme division.

Originally conceived as an app that could track a user’s physical wellbeing(pulse , heart rate etc) to detect emotional responses to content, it later evolved through client and expert feedback into a simpler, more realistic platform emphasizing context control, conversation quality, and user awareness by exposing users to more content outside their echo chambers
